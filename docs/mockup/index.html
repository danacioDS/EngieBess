    About

    A concept prototype submitted with the proposal for ENGIE's BESS Operational and Financial Modeling Platform (RFP-264144-1). It answers one question: how much is a behind-the-meter battery in PJM worth, where does that value come from, and which assumptions hold it up? Data and prices are synthetic and dispatch is simplified; the purpose is to make the engineering design visible and testable, not to produce investment-grade numbers.

    A 3-minute tour

    Step 1Change an assumption

    Raise the power rating or the capacity price. Everything recalculates, and the strip at the top shows the change from the baseline.

    Step 2See the battery operate

    Pick July and a peak weekday: the peak target, the coincident peak hour, charging at night and RegD in idle hours.

    Step 3Follow the money

    Bill savings come from the tariff engine; RegD revenue comes from PJM settlement and is then valued in Financial.

    Step 4Check the reasoning

    Which engineering rules pass on this run, and which working assumptions wait for ENGIE's confirmation.

    Engineering foundation

    The prototype follows the engineering baseline in the repository (Stage A definition, Stage B architecture)
    System context→Data→BESS and load & market→Operational requirements→Dispatch→Degradation→Financial→Scenarios and results
    Physical-operational cycle

    Time-dependent and stateful: dispatch drives cycling, cycling drives state of health, and each year's state of health sets next year's capacity and degradation signal.

    Economic-financial cycle

    Consumes the operational results (bill savings, settled market value, degradation events) and discounts them over the project horizon.
